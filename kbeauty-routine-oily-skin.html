A 5-step K-beauty routine for oily skin

More layers, less oil — the Korean approach works by balancing sebum instead of stripping it, which is why it holds up better long-term than harsh, drying routines.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. If you have persistent acne, rosacea, or another diagnosed skin condition, check in with a dermatologist before changing your routine.

Why oily skin still needs a full routine

It feels counterintuitive to add products to skin that already produces too much oil. But over-cleansing and skipping moisturizer often makes oiliness worse, not better — when skin senses it's losing moisture, it tends to produce more sebum to compensate. Korean routines are built to hydrate lightly at every step, which is what actually keeps oil production in check over time.

Start with the essentials below. A cleanser, moisturizer, and sunscreen are enough on their own — everything else is a layer you add once you're consistent with the basics.

1

Low-pH gel cleanser

Cleanse with a gentle, low-pH formula that doesn't leave skin feeling tight. A tight, squeaky-clean feeling usually means the cleanser stripped too much and skin will overcompensate with more oil later in the day.

Look for: gel or foam texture, fragrance-free, low-pH (around 5–6)

Try: COSRX Low pH Good Morning Gel Cleanser

2

Hydrating toner

Korean toners are hydrating, not the alcohol-based astringents many people associate with the word. This step preps skin to absorb what comes next and adds a first light layer of moisture.

Look for: water-based, no alcohol, no added fragrance

Try: Mixsoon Glacier Water Hyaluronic Acid Toner

3

Niacinamide serum

Niacinamide is one of the better-studied ingredients for oil control. Clinical research on a 2% concentration found it measurably reduced sebum output after four weeks of consistent twice-daily use, without drying out skin. Most people tolerate 2–5% well; higher concentrations exist but have less independent evidence behind the extra strength.

Look for: 2–5% niacinamide, fragrance-free

Try: The Ordinary Niacinamide 10% + Zinc 1% or Good Molecules Niacinamide Serum

4

Lightweight, non-comedogenic moisturizer

This is the step people with oily skin are most likely to skip — and the one that matters most. A gel-cream texture hydrates without adding heaviness, and consistent moisturizer use is part of what keeps sebum production from spiking.

Look for: gel-cream texture, fragrance-free, labeled non-comedogenic

Try: Beauty of Joseon Matte Sun Stick-adjacent line's gel moisturizer, or Purito Wonder Releaf Centella Serum layered as a light moisturizer step

5

Sunscreen, SPF 30+

The one non-negotiable step. Sunscreen goes on last, every morning, regardless of skin type. Korean sunscreens are widely liked for oily skin specifically because many formulas feel lightweight and matte rather than greasy.

Look for: broad-spectrum, SPF 30 or higher, matte or lightweight finish

Try: Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics SPF 50

If you only take one thing from this: consistency with four or five simple products outperforms an elaborate routine you abandon after a week.

What to add later, once this is consistent

Once the basics are steady for a few weeks, optional additions like a chemical exfoliant (AHA/BHA), a treatment serum for specific concerns, or a sheet mask can layer in — always one new active at a time, so you can tell what's actually working.
