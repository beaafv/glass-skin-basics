Routine for visible pores on nose and cheeks

Pores can't be shrunk permanently, but oil and congestion inside them can — which is what actually changes how visible they look.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Introduce one new active at a time.

Why the nose and cheeks specifically

The T-zone produces more oil than the rest of the face, even on skin that's otherwise dry or combination — which is part of why pores look more prominent there. Nose pores also take more physical stress day to day: touching, glasses, blowing your nose. Cheek pores are affected by things like sleeping position, and sun exposure tends to hit the nose and cheeks more directly than the forehead, which accelerates the collagen loss that makes pores look stretched over time. This unevenness is normal and doesn't mean anything is wrong with your skin specifically.

Three ingredients that do most of the work

1

Salicylic acid (BHA)

BHA is oil-soluble, which means it can get inside the pore itself and break down the oil and debris causing the congestion — this is why it's the ingredient most associated with visibly clearing blackheads specifically, rather than just working on the skin's surface.

Try: COSRX BHA Blackhead Power Liquid

2

Niacinamide

Where BHA clears what's already congesting the pore, niacinamide works on why it refills — regulating oil production over time. It also has anti-inflammatory and barrier-supporting properties, so it calms the redness that often shows up around congested areas.

Try: Anua Niacinamide 10 + TXA 4 Serum

3

Azelaic acid

A gentler alternative or add-on to BHA — azelaic acid targets redness and uneven texture around congested pores while being generally well tolerated, including on reactive or easily-irritated skin. It pairs well with hyaluronic acid formulas that keep the treatment from feeling drying.

Try: Anua Azelaic Acid 10 Hyaluron Redness Soothing Serum

How to build the routine

Start BHA at two to three times a week in the evening, applied to clean, dry skin — give it 10-15 minutes to work before layering anything else. If you're new to BHA, expect a short adjustment period; some purging (temporary breakouts as buildup clears) is common in the first couple of weeks. Once your skin tolerates it, niacinamide layers in after — morning and evening both work, though many people find applying it after BHA in the evening routine simplest.

You don't need to treat the whole face the same way. If pores are mainly a T-zone and cheek issue, it's fine to focus product application there rather than spreading a full dose evenly across skin that isn't congested.

Pores don't shrink overnight, and this isn't a routine with a dramatic before/after in week one — most people need 8-12 weeks of consistent use before the change is clearly visible.

What else affects how pores look

Daily SPF matters here more than it might seem — sun damage accelerates the collagen loss that makes pores look larger and more oval over time, so skipping sunscreen undercuts everything else in this routine. Over-cleansing and harsh physical scrubs work against you too; irritated skin swells slightly, which can make pores look more prominent, not less.
